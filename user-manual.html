Overview

Pecking Order is a local-first record-keeping app for poultry keepers and breeders. It brings chicken and duck profiles, egg collection, groups, breeding records, chick batches, genealogy, custom fields and pecking-order relationships together.

Core flock-management tools are free. Optional one-time packs and breeder purchases provide additional capacity or features. Plus includes breeder tools, Breeding Projects, cloud and web access while active. Version 0.20.0 and later also allows verified event previews to link web access until their original expiry. You do not need to subscribe to use locally purchased breeder features.

Local-first app: your flock records are stored on your device. Pecking Order does not require an account to use the main record-keeping features.
Latest test update: pen headcounts, the Home and Full Breeder tiers, deworming reminders, automatic sync and record history are being introduced through Google Play closed testing. The public Android and iPhone versions may not yet include them. The sections below identify these additions; purchase availability and prices are shown in your store.

Some controls may look slightly different between Android, iPhone and the web app, and optional features depend on your plan.

New starting points and recovery tools

These controls are included in the next mobile update. Follow worked flock and breeding examples, or use the printable club/show introduction.

  1. Choose your first task. First-use setup offers Meet my flock, Record eggs or Start a hatch. Try a sample flock opens a fictional, isolated demonstration; it never writes to your real records. It is also available in Settings.
  2. Choose your opening screen. On phones, Today shows the existing dashboard: egg trends, project links and the next pending task. From version 0.20.1, Today opens by default unless you have chosen otherwise. Turn off Open on Today in Settings to start in My Flock. In 0.20.0, turn it on to use Today. The dashboard egg chart opens at the newest dates; scroll back for earlier days.
  3. Add several birds. In My flock, open More bird actions → Add several birds. Quick entry lets you enter names/codes with shared species, sex and breed. CSV accepts name, code, species, sex, breed, hatch_date (YYYY-MM-DD), and status. Review each row before saving; invalid and duplicate rows are flagged. Existing birds are never overwritten. If the selected set exceeds your allowance, none are added. Retained inactive full records count too.
  4. Manage saved files. Settings → Storage & recovery lists app-generated backups, recovery copies and exports by size/date. Save or share a copy before deleting it. Restore creates a Before restore recovery copy before replacing data; use Restore this copy to return to it. On the web, clearing browser storage also removes local recovery files, so download important copies.
  5. Get useful help. Settings → Help & diagnostics prepares a status-only report you can read before sharing. It excludes account details, record content and purchase proofs. Suggest a missing breed opens an email draft; you can already enter a custom breed.

Getting started

  1. Open Pecking Order.
  2. On first use, name your flock (or keep Main Flock), acknowledge that records are stored on this device, then tap Create flock and continue.
  3. On Flock, tap the Add bird icon in the top bar. Choose Chicken or Duck, enter a name and any details you know, then save.
  4. Tap that bird in the list to open its profile. You can edit it later; you do not need to complete every field now.
  5. Use the Record eggs card on Flock to add your first daily collection if you want to track eggs.
  6. After you have entered records you care about, open Settings → Create & share backup and save the file outside the app.
Start small: a flock name and one bird are enough to begin. Add groups, breeding history and relationship observations as you learn them.

Tracking a whole pen? In the latest test version, Full Breeder also lets you start at Flocks → Create Flock → Pen / flock headcount. Enter a number of birds without making an individual profile for each one. You can use both approaches together.

Web dashboard

Sign in at app.projectpeckingorder.com with the same email as the linked phone. Use the left menu on a computer or the menu button on a smaller screen.

  1. Check the sync banner. If records are waiting or conflicting, open History & sync.
  2. Read Needs attention & coming up. It shows one next step per active project or separate hatch/batch, with overdue or due work clearly highlighted. Open it for all checks.
  3. Review the egg chart for the last 14 days. A blank day means not recorded; it is not a zero.
  4. Open an active project or use Record eggs or Start a hatch.

The dashboard displays due work; seeing it there does not prove a phone notification was delivered. Phone permissions and device settings still apply.

My Flock

Flock → My Flock is where you manage individual chicken and duck records. Tap Add bird in the top bar to create a record, or tap an existing bird to open its profile and choose Edit Bird.

Use the Flock selector to show one flock or all flocks, then choose All, Chickens or Ducks. Sort birds by offers alphabetical, pecking order, egg colour, egg quantity, age and custom-field options. A pecking-order sort is only a useful list view of recorded observations; real dominance can include loops and cannot always be reduced to an exact first-to-last rank.

Bird profile details

A bird profile can include:

  • Name and/or your own bird code, such as a leg-band number
  • Sex
  • Record type
  • Status
  • Breed
  • Egg colour
  • Hatch date and age
  • Notes
  • Photo
  • Custom field values, when custom fields are set up

Use Settings → Show bird codes first to put your ring or band codes before names. Codes are labels you choose; database identifiers are not intended as bird labels. New installs start with ducks hidden; enable them in the flock settings when needed. Existing duck preferences are retained.

Full bird records

A full bird record is for a chicken or duck that is part of your current or historical flock records. Full records count toward the allowance for that species.

Ancestor-only records

An ancestor-only record is for an external or historical bird that you want to include in genealogy without treating it as part of your living flock. For example, you might use this for a known parent, grandparent or purchased breeding-line bird.

Status

Bird status helps keep your flock list accurate while preserving useful history. Status options may include active, sold, rehomed, deceased or missing.

Custom fields

Open Settings → Custom Fields to name the extra details you want on chicken records, such as leg band or bloodline. The free plan has two fields; field packs or Breeder access increase the allowance.

Bird and egg photos

Photos help identify birds and make graph views easier to understand.

  • Use Add photo when creating or editing a bird.
  • Use Replace photo to update an existing photo.
  • Use Remove to remove the photo from that bird profile.

In the latest test version, Hobby includes one main bird photo. Home Breeder includes four photos per bird: three bird photos and one egg photo. Full Breeder includes eight: seven bird photos and one egg photo, plus up to eight photos per pen. Eligible original Breeder Unlock purchases retain Full Breeder access. Active Plus access includes Full Breeder photo allowances. After a downgrade or the end of timed access, existing photos remain saved; adding or replacing protected slots requires the corresponding Breeder access.

Use Add another photo in a pen to add optional gallery images. Update all devices before syncing additional photo slots. If a device requests an update, its local records and cloud history are kept.

New and replaced photos are copied into app storage when the record is saved, so they can be included in backups.

Older photos linked from your gallery may be migrated into app storage the next time that bird record is saved.

Egg collection and charts

Record a daily collection

  1. On Flock, tap Record eggs.
  2. Use Change date if you are entering an earlier collection.
  3. Set the number of Chicken eggs and Duck eggs, then tap Add this collection to selected day. Each save updates that day's total.
  4. For a correction, find the entry under Collection history and open its actions menu to Edit or Delete. Turn off Selected date only to see other dates.

Read the chart

Tap View egg statistics. Choose Day (last 30 days), Week (last 12 weeks) or Year (monthly totals), then choose Combined, Chicken or Duck. The latest bars appear on the right; scroll sideways for older dates. View graph as a list gives a text alternative. Days without a collection count as zero in the displayed averages.

Updated builds also offer bar or line charts and a coloured rolling-average line in Chart options. The rolling average follows the trend over time. You can record defective eggs separately and choose whether to include them in totals. If you do not keep ducks, hide duck options in Settings and turn them on again later.

Defective eggs, notes and export

Open Defective eggs and notes below the regular count. The next mobile update gives this panel shading, an outline and an edit icon. Enter defective eggs separately from regular eggs, choose a reason if useful, and add an optional note. The totals preference changes statistics, not the stored counts.

From Egg statistics, choose a date range and select Export this date range as CSV. This egg-history export is free. It includes missing-day markers, chicken and duck counts, defects and notes; wider breeder exports are a separate paid feature.

The automatic trend is 7 days for the daily view, 28 days for 12 weeks and 90 days for the year view. Grouped charts show eggs per recorded day. The average uses recorded days only, including explicit zero days. It describes your records, not eggs that may have been collected on missing days. You can adjust or turn off the trend in Chart options.

Other egg observations

From an eligible bird's profile, Record individual egg observation records that bird's output for sorting and history. These observations do not add to the overall daily collection total. The Egg collection screen also offers observations by breed with active Breeder access. Saved breed observations remain visible if timed access ends, but adding new ones requires Breeder access.

In Settings, you can optionally turn on the Daily egg reminder and choose its time. Your phone must allow notifications for it to appear.

Groups and flock organisation

Open Flocks to create or rename a flock or create a quarantine group. Groups help organise birds in a way that matches how you actually manage them. A quarantine group is a location, not a life status.

Examples of groups include:

  • Main flock
  • Breeding pen
  • Rooster group
  • Grow-out group
  • Quarantine group
  • Sold or historical group

Track a pen by headcount — latest test version

Full Breeder adds a second way to organise a flock. A pen can represent a numbered enclosure, a breed or another group you manage together. Your existing individual bird records remain available.

  1. Open Flocks → Create Flock.
  2. Choose Pen / flock headcount. Choose Individual birds instead if you want a group of named or numbered bird profiles.
  3. Enter a Pen or flock name and species, with an optional breed.
  4. Enter Birds tracked together and the Count date. You can also add a photo and notes, then save.
  5. Open the pen to use Update headcount / move birds, Record eggs for this pen, Record breeding / hatch or its Deworming reminder.

Record additions, sales, losses, transfers and count corrections as changes to the pen. The movement history explains how the headcount changed. Moving birds between pens updates both sides of the transfer.

Use Track a bird individually when you want a profile for a particular bird. If it is already part of the pen count, leave Already included in this pen’s headcount selected so it is removed from the birds counted together. The named bird and remaining pen count are shown separately, avoiding a duplicate count.

On a bird, Current location (primary flock) determines where it is counted. Secondary group memberships organise birds without changing that primary count. Moving to Quarantine removes the current primary membership; returning sets Main Flock as the current location.

In the browser, use the flock report control on a flock or pen to download a PDF report with its birds, headcount and available records. Reports reflect the records currently saved on that device; sync first when you need changes from another device.

Lead hen

On Order, use Lead Bird to nominate a lead hen or bird for the selected group. The star marks this role on the map. It does not automatically create a dominance relationship or prove that the bird outranks every other bird.

Breeding records and chick batches

Pecking Order includes tools for recording breeding activity and chick outcomes. The Chick batches shortcut is in the top bar of Flock.

Breeding records

Use breeding records to keep notes about pairings, breeding plans, fertility, hatch results or other breeding details relevant to your flock.

Chick batches

Chick batches let you record a hatch or group of chicks before every chick becomes an individual chicken record.

A chick batch may be used to track:

  • Batch name
  • Known mother and father, where applicable
  • Number of chicks
  • Notes
  • Outcomes such as mortality, rehoming, sale or another reduction

Converting chicks into individual records

When a chick becomes important enough to track individually, you can create a full chicken record from the batch. Known batch parents can be linked automatically into the family tree.

Move chicks into a pen — latest test version

With Full Breeder, use Move chicks into pen from a pen or the corresponding action on a chick batch. Choose the number to move. The app records the batch allocation and adds those birds to the pen headcount, without requiring a name for each chick. Only chicks still available in the batch can be moved.

Guided hatch monitoring

Basic chick batches remain available on the Hobby plan. Breeder access adds guided hatch monitoring; a project-linked hatch requires Plus. On the web use Hatches & chicks. On the phone reach it from My Flock.

  1. Start a hatch. Name the hatch, choose chicken or duck eggs (when ducks are enabled), select the incubation method and enter the number of eggs and the actual set date. Link known parents or leave unknown parents unassigned.
  2. Check the suggested dates. Chicken eggs default to 21 days after setting, most ducks to 28 days, and Muscovy ducks to 35 days. Set day is day zero. You can change the expected date for your birds or incubation circumstances.
  3. Follow the countdown. The large number shows days until expected hatch. It changes emphasis near hatch and on/after the due date. Expected dates are estimates, not guarantees.
  4. Use reminders and optional checks. Guided hatch reminders start on. Turn the switch off or on without opening a form; use Change dates to adjust dates. Chicken candling reminders are at days 7, 10 and 14. Candling is optional and never blocks progress. Tick a check to record completion; add photos or notes separately when useful. Chicken hatch preparation and stopping incubator turning are suggested at day 18. Duck schedules adapt to the egg type and expected date. Follow the care requirements for your birds and equipment.
  5. Record hatch. Enter the total hatched so far, including any chicks recorded earlier. Choose whether hatching is still in progress or finished. Updating the total avoids creating a new batch for every chick. A linked chick batch holds the growing chicks.
  6. Watch chicks grow. Record losses or corrections, link individual birds when identifiable, and retain the connection to the original hatch. Bought or received chicks can enter directly as a chick batch without inventing an incubation history.

Phone notification permission is required. Check that it is allowed after reinstalling, changing phones or restoring data. Web pages show the timeline and due work; do not rely on them to send phone alerts. Keep your normal physical checks and care routine.

Breeding Projects: a complete workflow

Plus subscription · In Development. Open Breeding projects from the web menu or My Flock on the phone. The Journey view is the standard approach. Turn on Advanced details only when you need the extra planning and assessment fields.

  1. Create a project and a measurable goal. For example, “Our green-egg flock” with a goal to record consistent shell colour. Start with one or two goals you can actually observe. Add a note explaining what would count as success.
  2. Choose the birds. Add existing birds by name or code. Record whether each is a founder, candidate, selected breeder or reference. Keep unknown parentage unknown. Add reference photos rather than relying on memory.
  3. Start a hatch inside the project. Select the parents, set date, eggs and method. This creates a linked generation plan and incubation record. You can also link an existing chick batch. Each hatch has its own journey, so several generations or families can run alongside each other.
  4. Follow the open stage. The current stage expands as you progress: Choose birds and set eggs → Incubate → Record hatch → Watch chicks grow → Review results. Earlier stages remain available for corrections, and later stages can be previewed. Optional checks do not hold the journey back.
  5. Record what you notice. Select a bird, batch or the whole project, the generation and the life stage. Describe what you observed and compare it with a goal. Use “Not assessed” when a chick is too young to judge. Fertility figures and extra measurements are optional.
  6. Add photos at useful stages. Use Photos for references, or attach images to an assessment. Include a caption, date and stage where relevant. Use consistent lighting and a physical colour reference for egg or plumage comparisons. Photos support observations; they do not prove genotype.
  7. Review, report and select. Open Report to review goals, plans, observations and photographs across generations. Keep evidence separate from inference. Select suitable breeders and start the next generation when ready; consider health, relatedness and available space as well as the target trait.
  8. Finish or archive. Use the project’s archive control when it should leave the active list. Find it in archived projects and restore it if needed. Deleting project records requires confirmation; read what remains linked before proceeding. Existing hatches and their care reminders continue independently of archiving.

Example 1: a first family hatch

Create “Spring hatch” with the purpose Hatch and raise chicks. Choose the parents, set 12 chicken eggs on 1 November, and check the suggested 22 November hatch date. Optional candling dates are 8, 11 and 15 November; preparation and stopping incubator turning are suggested for 19 November. Tick checks you actually did; leave the others unrecorded. If five chicks hatch on 22 November and two more on 23 November, record 5 first, then update the total to 7. Mark hatching finished when appropriate. Add growth photos to the linked batch, then create or link individual bird profiles as they become identifiable.

Example 2: work towards greener eggs

Start with a goal that describes the shell colour you want to observe. Record parent backgrounds as known or inferred, then hatch a generation. At chick and growing stages assess health and other visible traits; leave egg colour unassessed. At laying age, photograph eggs under comparable conditions and record which hen laid them when known. Compare results before choosing the next breeders. Roosters do not lay eggs; evidence about the eggs of their relatives or offspring is indirect. Results can vary within a line, and a photograph or a breed name cannot guarantee what the next generation will produce.

Example 3: reduce an unwanted visible trait

Create a goal such as reduced leg feathering. Record the parents and several observations as offspring mature. Separate “not yet assessed” from “outside target”. Use the report to compare families and generations before selecting breeders. For a suspected inherited health condition, record the concern and seek appropriate veterinary or genetics advice rather than treating an app suggestion as a diagnosis.

A short testing checklist

  • Create a project, select coded birds and start its hatch.
  • Confirm the expected date follows the species and set date.
  • Toggle reminders without a new page; tick an optional check without adding notes.
  • Record a partial hatch, then increase the total and finish it. Confirm one linked batch.
  • Add a stage photo and observation, and find both in the report.
  • Check that the dashboard shows only the project’s next task.
  • Archive the project, confirm it leaves the active list, then restore it.
  • Save, sync, and check the same records on your second device.

Family tree, ancestors and pedigree

Use the Tree tab to choose a chicken and see its parents and offspring. Full flock birds and ancestor-only records can both be linked; former and deceased birds remain part of the history.

Assigning parents

Open a chicken profile and choose Family Tree / Parents, or select a chicken on the Tree tab and choose Edit Parents. You can create an ancestor-only record when a known parent is not part of your own flock.

Ancestor-only records

Ancestor-only records are useful when a parent or grandparent is not part of your current flock but should still appear in genealogy.

Pedigree depth

The standard visual pedigree supports a useful hobby-level family tree. Breeder Unlock extends the interactive pedigree to more generations.

Share pedigree PDF

With Breeder access, open a chicken profile and use Share pedigree PDF to create a shareable profile with photo, details, parents and grandparents.

Pecking Order flock map

The Pecking Order area records observed relationships and displays them on a flock map. The map is based on what you record; it is not a calculated ranking of every bird.

Record what you have observed

  1. Open Order and choose a group in View group.
  2. Choose Shared order or Duck-only order.
  3. Tap Add Relationship and choose the birds and the relationship you observed. Add further observations over time; you do not need to map every possible pair.
  4. Tap the large Pecking Order button to see the complete flock map.

Dominance relationships

A dominance relationship records that one bird is dominant over another. These relationships are used to build the pecking-order graph.

Best friends

Best-friend links are separate from dominance. Matching coloured and lettered hearts identify friends on the map without changing the order; selected birds can show the direct connection.

Reading and arranging the map

All shows the complete recorded order. Arrows point from the dominant bird to the bird that yields. Where lines cross, a small bridge means a crossing, not a relationship. Tap a bird to highlight its direct relationships; tap its name above the map for a written list, or clear the selection to return to the complete view. Pinch to zoom and drag the background to pan.

Choose Arrange to move birds without changing relationships. Done saves their positions and Cancel discards the changes. The menu lets you switch between Auto layout and My layout without deleting your arrangement; the older Network layout remains available separately.

Observed loops

Sometimes real flocks do not form a straight hierarchy. When recorded dominance relationships circle back, a Loop button traces that cycle on the map without moving the birds. Loops may share birds. Select All to return to the complete map.

Snapshots

On Order, choose Save Snapshot to preserve a group's recorded order at a point in time. Open Snapshots to review them later. Snapshots are read-only, so later changes to live relationships do not rewrite an older snapshot.

Share and save graph images

Use the share or save controls to export a map image for records, comparison or social sharing. Larger flocks can be easier to inspect in the exported image than on a phone screen.

Backup and restore

A backup is a .peckingorder file containing your database and app-managed media. It can include bird records, chick batches, genealogy, egg records, order snapshots and game scores. Latest test builds also include pen records, pen photos, headcount movements and deworming records. Store purchases are tied to the store account and are restored separately; importing a backup does not grant another account’s purchases.

Create and share backup

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Choose Create & share backup.
  3. Use the share screen to save the backup somewhere safe, such as Files, Drive, email or another device. Check that you can find the saved file before relying on it.

Restore backup

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Choose Restore backup.
  3. Select a valid .peckingorder backup file.
  4. Read the replacement warning and confirm only if this is the backup you want.
  5. In the latest test version, tap Load restored data after the restore completes. If your older version asks you to close and reopen, follow that notice.
Restoring replaces current app data. Restore only when you are sure the selected backup is the one you want to use.

Settings also has a backup and new-features reminder. It is on by default where notifications are allowed and repeats about every 30 days. Turning it off and on starts a new interval. This reminder itself does not create a backup. Optional automatic cloud backup is a separate setting.

Automatic cloud sync and history

Latest test version. Plus provides cloud backup and web access. Version 0.20.0 and later also accepts an eligible store-verified event preview for its remaining original 30-day period. A permanent Home or Full Breeder purchase alone does not include ongoing cloud access. Signing in does not start a subscription. See event-preview linking instructions.

Connect existing phone records

  1. Update to a supported phone build, then open Settings → Plus, cloud & web access.
  2. Sign in with your email and the emailed code. Keep the code private and enter it in the app.
  3. Activate or restore the cloud subscription using the store account that purchased it.
  4. Choose Link the records on this device and confirm the account. This is your consent to sync the existing records and their managed photos.
  5. Open History & sync and let the first sync finish. On your computer, open the web app and sign in using the same email.

What saves automatically?

Finish each entry using its usual Save or Record control. There is no separate “save to cloud” step: recorded changes sync within a few seconds while connected. Changes waiting to upload remain on that device if you close the app or browser and sync when you open it again. Unfinished forms are not saved records.

Each device checks for newer records when you open or return to the app, save a change, or choose History & sync → Sync now. While open, the web app also checks for updates about every 30 seconds. Changes received while you are editing wait until you finish that screen. Phone records remain usable offline. Let the first device finish uploading, then open, resume or manually sync the second device before relying on its copy.

Go back after a mistake

Open History & sync → Cloud history to see the latest 50 saved record versions. Use Restore beside the version you need and read the confirmation before continuing. A restore makes a new current version and can replace changes made since the chosen version. It keeps the earlier history rather than rewriting it. Photos used by retained versions remain available with those versions.

Separate archive backups keep up to five completed copies of your database and managed photos. In Plus, cloud & web access, Automatic daily backup creates one when you open or return to the phone app after at least 24 hours. Record history and archive backups serve different recovery needs; you can also keep an exported backup outside the service.

If two devices change the same information

Changes to different records or fields can merge automatically. If both devices changed the same value before syncing, History & sync shows readable entries for review. Choose the value to keep for each conflicting entry, then apply the choices. Unrelated changes still merge. The current interface resolves the conflicting values rather than replacing every record. Recovery copies are kept. A successful full backup is separate from live sync; check its status if the other device has not updated.

Signing out stops automatic sync on that device. It does not delete your phone records or your cloud copies. See the privacy policy for storage, history, photo retention and account deletion details.

Deworming reminders

Latest test version. Open Settings → Deworming reminders. Reminders work locally without a cloud subscription. The free six-month reminder starts enabled; your phone must also allow notifications.

PlanReminder choices
FreeTurn the general six-month reminder on or off.
Home BreederTurn the general reminder on or off and choose three or six months.
Full BreederUse the general three- or six-month schedule, or set an individual pen’s interval from 1 to 120 whole months. Each pen can also be switched off.

For a pen, open Flocks → your pen → Deworming reminder. Leave Use general schedule on to follow your general setting, or turn it off to set the pen’s own interval. A custom pen schedule replaces its general reminder, so you do not get both for the same pen.

Tap Treatment completed to record the actual treatment date. The next due date is that date plus the chosen number of calendar months. If there is no recorded treatment, the interval starts from the date reminders were enabled. A general completion applies to pens using the general schedule; record custom-schedule pen treatments separately.

You can correct a mistaken treatment record in its history. Due dates follow the latest remaining treatment. Month-end dates are adjusted to the last day of a shorter month. Due reminders use 9 am local time, and an overdue item remains visible until you record treatment or change its schedule.

These are records and reminders for the schedule you choose, not advice about a product, dose or whether treatment is needed.

Event Breeder Preview

An event organiser may provide a single-use store code for a 30-day Breeder preview. Redeem it through Google Play or the App Store using Redeem event code on the Plans & Purchases screen. It ends without starting a paid subscription. Availability depends on the specific event's dates and region.

When timed Breeder access ends, existing flock data and extra photos remain saved, but adding or replacing content that requires Breeder access is restricted until you regain access. Keep regular backups of your records.

Restore purchases

Use Settings → Plans & Purchases → Restore purchases if you reinstall the app or move to another device using the same store account. A data backup restores records; the store restores eligible purchases. Purchases from one store are not transferred by the backup to the other store.

Current Plus entitlement: Plus includes all Home and Full Breeder tools, Breeding Projects with stage photos and reports, cloud backup, sync and web access while active. One-time Home/Full purchases do not include cloud or subscription-only Projects. If Plus ends, your separately purchased one-time tools remain available. Existing records are not a reason to start a new purchase; restore the original store purchase first.

Keeping your records safe

Pecking Order is local-first. Without linked cloud access, your records stay on the device unless you export a backup. With supported builds and linked cloud access, recorded changes sync automatically while connected and earlier record versions are kept in cloud history. Create separate backups of important records and save them outside the app as well.

Do not rely only on the phone. A backup left only on the same phone will not protect you from phone loss, damage or replacement. Restoring a backup replaces the app's current local data, so keep a copy of the current data before restoring an older file.

App-managed bird and pen photos can be included in backups. Older photos that still point to a gallery file may need that original file to be available; edit and save the bird record to migrate a reachable older photo before making a new backup.

Troubleshooting

I cannot see a purchase I made

Open Settings → Plans & Purchases and use Restore purchases. Make sure you are using the same Google Play or App Store account that made the purchase.

I cannot find pen mode

Check that you have the latest test version, then open Flocks → Create Flock → Pen / flock headcount. Pen mode requires Full Breeder, including the original full Breeder Unlock. An active Plus subscription also includes these tools. Home Breeder owners can use the Home-to-Full upgrade.

I signed in on the web but cannot see my phone records

Use the same email on both devices and a supported phone build. On the phone, open Plus, cloud & web access, check that cloud access is active and link the records on that device. Check History & sync for progress, a connection error or a conflict to resolve. Leave the phone app open until its first sync finishes; then return to the web app. Creating an account alone does not upload an existing phone database.

I changed something by mistake

For a small correction, edit the record normally. With cloud access, History & sync → Cloud history also lets you restore an earlier saved record version. Restoring changes the whole set of records, so check the date and confirmation carefully.

My backup is not useful if it is only on my phone

That is correct. After creating a backup, save it somewhere outside the app and outside the phone where possible.

My egg totals look higher or lower than the bird observations

Daily flock collections and individual bird observations are separate records. An individual observation helps sort birds and does not change the overall collection total. Edit the appropriate record in its own history.

Some older photos are missing from a backup

New and replaced photos are copied into app storage when saved. Very old gallery-linked photos may need the original file to still exist. Opening and saving the chicken record again may help migrate reachable older photos into app storage.

The pecking-order hierarchy does not look like a simple straight line

Real flock behaviour can be messy. If birds have circular or conflicting relationships, the app may show loops or network-style relationships rather than a simple top-to-bottom order.

I restored a backup and the app says to close and reopen

Close and reopen Pecking Order so the restored database is loaded cleanly.

Anonymous usage and app ratings

The updated app asks once whether you want to Share anonymous usage. Choose Share or Not now; nothing is sent before you agree. Change your choice later in Settings. This is a device/browser setting, separate from your cloud account.

Optional reports contain which features were used that day, ranges for active individual bird counts, chicken/duck mix and recognised breed categories. They exclude bird names, codes, free-text breeds, notes, photos and account details. A breed outside the built-in list is counted only as Other. Counts help identify useful features and catalog gaps; they are not a count of unique people. Read the full privacy details.

The next mobile update keeps a local count of consecutive days you open the app. After 15 days it may request a native store rating when you are back at the main screen. Missing a day restarts the streak. It makes at most one automatic request per installation, with no reward or penalty for rating. Apple or Google can suppress the prompt; TestFlight does not display a live review request. This streak is not uploaded and does not depend on agreeing to usage sharing.

In Flock Runner on the web, the up arrow jumps and the down arrow ducks. The games page also offers links to Boulder Slider and information about other games as they become available.
