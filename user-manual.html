1. Overview

Pecking Order is a local-first record-keeping app for poultry keepers and breeders. It brings chicken and duck profiles, egg collection, groups, breeding records, chick batches, genealogy, custom fields and pecking-order relationships together.

Core flock-management tools are free. Optional one-time packs, a permanent Breeder Unlock, a monthly subscription and limited event previews provide additional capacity or features.

Local-first app: your flock records are stored on your device. Pecking Order does not require an account to use the main record-keeping features.

2. Important: backups

Your records are valuable. Please create regular backups and save them somewhere outside the app, such as Files, cloud storage, email or another device.

Do not rely only on the phone. A backup left only on the same phone is not protection against phone loss, damage or replacement.

A Pecking Order backup can restore your app data when you move devices or need to recover records. It can include your database, chick batches, snapshots, genealogy, egg records, game scores and app-managed bird photos. Purchases must be restored through the store separately.

3. Getting started

  1. Open Pecking Order.
  2. On a fresh install, name your flock and accept the app terms if prompted.
  3. Add your first chicken or duck in My Flock.
  4. Add basic details such as name, sex, breed, hatch date, egg colour and notes.
  5. Add a photo if you want one shown in profiles and graph views.
  6. Create or use groups to organise birds by flock, pen, breeding group, quarantine or another system that suits you.
  7. Create a backup once you have entered important records.

You do not need to fill in every field at the start. Pecking Order is intended to grow with your records over time.

4. My Flock

My Flock is where you manage individual chicken and duck records.

Bird profile details

A bird profile can include:

  • Name
  • Sex
  • Record type
  • Status
  • Breed
  • Egg colour
  • Hatch date and age
  • Notes
  • Photo
  • Custom field values, when custom fields are set up

Full bird records

A full bird record is for a chicken or duck that is part of your current or historical flock records. Full records count toward the allowance for that species.

Ancestor-only records

An ancestor-only record is for an external or historical bird that you want to include in genealogy without treating it as part of your living flock. For example, you might use this for a known parent, grandparent or purchased breeding-line bird.

Status

Bird status helps keep your flock list accurate while preserving useful history. Status options may include active, sold, rehomed, deceased or missing.

5. Bird and egg photos

Photos help identify birds and make graph views easier to understand.

  • Use Add photo when creating or editing a bird.
  • Use Replace photo to update an existing photo.
  • Use Remove to remove the photo from that bird profile.

One main bird photo is available on the free plan. Bird photos 2 and 3 and the egg photo require active Breeder access (permanent Unlock, monthly subscription or an event preview). If timed access ends, existing photos stay saved, but you cannot add or replace the extra photos until access returns.

New and replaced photos are copied into app storage when the record is saved, so they can be included in backups.

Older photos linked from your gallery may be migrated into app storage the next time that bird record is saved.

6. Egg collection and charts

Use Egg collection to record daily chicken and duck egg totals. You can review and correct past entries in the history. The statistics screen shows collection trends over time; use its date or range controls to inspect recent entries.

Detailed egg entries by breed are a Breeder feature. Existing entries remain in your records if monthly or preview access ends, but adding further detailed entries requires active Breeder access.

In Settings, you can optionally turn on a daily egg reminder and choose its time. Your phone must allow notifications for the reminder to appear.

7. Groups and flock organisation

Groups help organise birds in a way that matches how you actually manage your flock.

Examples of groups include:

  • Main flock
  • Breeding pen
  • Rooster group
  • Grow-out group
  • Quarantine group
  • Sold or historical group

Lead hen

A lead hen can be selected as a separate group role. This is useful for describing the group, but it does not automatically create dominance relationships in the pecking-order graph.

8. Breeding records and chick batches

Pecking Order includes tools for recording breeding activity and chick outcomes.

Breeding records

Use breeding records to keep notes about pairings, breeding plans, fertility, hatch results or other breeding details relevant to your flock.

Chick batches

Chick batches let you record a hatch or group of chicks before every chick becomes an individual chicken record.

A chick batch may be used to track:

  • Batch name
  • Known mother and father, where applicable
  • Number of chicks
  • Notes
  • Outcomes such as mortality, rehoming, sale or another reduction

Converting chicks into individual records

When a chick becomes important enough to track individually, you can create a full chicken record from the batch. Known batch parents can be linked automatically into the family tree.

9. Family tree, ancestors and pedigree

The family tree tools help record known parentage and breeding lines.

Assigning parents

Open a chicken profile and use Family Tree / Parents to assign known parents.

Ancestor-only records

Ancestor-only records are useful when a parent or grandparent is not part of your current flock but should still appear in genealogy.

Pedigree depth

The standard visual pedigree supports a useful hobby-level family tree. Breeder Unlock extends the interactive pedigree to more generations.

Share pedigree PDF

Breeder Unlock includes a shareable pedigree PDF feature. From a chicken profile, use Share pedigree PDF to create a shareable profile with photo, details, parents and grandparents.

10. Pecking Order flock map

The Pecking Order area records observed relationships and displays them on a flock map. The map is based on what you record; it is not a calculated ranking of every bird.

Dominance relationships

A dominance relationship records that one bird is dominant over another. These relationships are used to build the pecking-order graph.

Best friends

Best-friend links are separate from dominance relationships. They can help show social bonds without changing the hierarchy.

Reading and arranging the map

All shows the complete recorded order. Arrows point from the dominant bird to the bird that yields. Tap a bird to highlight its direct relationships; tap its name above the map for a written list, or clear the selection to return to the complete view. Pinch to zoom and drag the background to pan.

Choose Arrange to move birds without changing their relationships. Done saves your positions and Cancel discards them. The menu lets you switch between automatic and your saved layout; the older Network layout remains available separately.

Observed loops

Sometimes real flocks do not form a straight hierarchy. When recorded dominance relationships circle back, a Loop button traces that cycle on the map without moving the birds. Loops may share birds. Select All to return to the complete map.

Snapshots

Snapshots preserve a saved view of a group’s pecking order at a point in time. This is useful because flock relationships can change over time.

Share and save graph images

Use the share or save controls to export a map image for records, comparison or social sharing. Larger flocks can be easier to inspect in the exported image than on a phone screen.

11. Backup and restore

Create and share backup

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Choose Create & share backup.
  3. Use the share screen to save the backup somewhere safe, such as Files, Drive, email or another device.

Restore backup

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Choose Restore backup.
  3. Select a valid Pecking Order backup file.
  4. Confirm that you want to replace the current app data.
  5. Close and reopen Pecking Order after the restore notice appears.
Restoring replaces current app data. Restore only when you are sure the selected backup is the one you want to use.

Settings also has a backup and new-features notification. It is on by default where notifications are allowed and repeats about every 30 days. You can turn it off or on again; re-enabling starts a new 30-day interval.

Monthly Breeder Access

This auto-renewing subscription gives the same Breeder features while active. Manage or cancel renewal through the store that billed you. Cancelling renewal does not immediately remove access already paid for; the store determines its end date.

Event Breeder Preview

An event organiser may provide a single-use store code for a 30-day Breeder preview. Redeem it through Google Play or the App Store using Redeem event code on the Plans & Purchases screen. It ends without starting a paid subscription. Availability depends on the specific event's dates and region.

When timed Breeder access ends, existing flock data and extra photos remain saved, but adding or replacing content that requires Breeder access is restricted until you regain access. Keep regular backups of your records.

Restore purchases

Use Restore purchases in Plans & Purchases if you reinstall the app or move to another device using the same store account.

13. Troubleshooting

I cannot see a purchase I made

Open Settings → Plans & Purchases and use Restore purchases. Make sure you are using the same Google Play or App Store account that made the purchase.

My backup is not useful if it is only on my phone

That is correct. After creating a backup, save it somewhere outside the app and outside the phone where possible.

Some older photos are missing from a backup

New and replaced photos are copied into app storage when saved. Very old gallery-linked photos may need the original file to still exist. Opening and saving the chicken record again may help migrate reachable older photos into app storage.

The pecking-order hierarchy does not look like a simple straight line

Real flock behaviour can be messy. If birds have circular or conflicting relationships, the app may show loops or network-style relationships rather than a simple top-to-bottom order.

I restored a backup and the app says to close and reopen

Close and reopen Pecking Order so the restored database is loaded cleanly.
